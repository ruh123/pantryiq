| # | Decision | Alternative rejected | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Airflow + GE in **isolated PEP-735 groups** | all deps in the main set | Airflow pins click/SQLAlchemy/pydantic narrowly and GE drags in pandas; CI runs `uv sync --locked`, so a resolver conflict would break lint and tests for unrelated work. | 3 | **Per-recipe + per-100g**, servings only where stated | estimating servings from mass | An estimated denominator would sit inside every per-serving number the agent ever quotes. Only 13.6% of recipes state one; the rest stay NULL. |

| 5 | Keep the **5 kcal floor**, relabel the metric, publish its sensitivity | drop it to 2 kcal | The floor is defensible (near-zero foods), but the metric was labelled "within 10%" while crediting rows at 18–28% error. Relabelled to "within 10% **or** 5 kcal/100g" with a sensitivity table. |

## 2. Phase 2's final review — four reviewers, seven defects (§13)

Three local agents (statistical methodology, code correctness, test quality) plus a cloud
review. **Three of the four independently found the same crash**, which is the strongest signal
the fan-out produced.

The methodology held up under adversarial review — the `max()` denominator does *not* flatter the
headline (all three reasonable definitions give identical results), Krippendorff's α matched an
independent reimplementation to four decimals, and the CI is a within-stratum bootstrap rather
than the misapplied Wilson interval I went looking for.

What was wrong:

1. `adjudicator_value.py` crashed on every run — its own test stubbed a 5-tuple and pinned the
   obsolete contract, so the suite stayed green defending the bug.
2. Candidate ties broke on `set` iteration order; `rank=0` is the baseline arm of the headline.
3. Five parser artifacts — worst was `salt and pepper to` (479 occurrences, head stratum)
   resolving confidently to tomato chili sauce at 92 kcal/100g.
4. §7's anchoring diagnostic measured a treatment never administered (40 of 42 "control" rows
   went to an annotator that never saw the control). **Retracted.**
5. §12's cosine-vs-curve AUC comparison is a monotone-transform identity, not a measurement.
6. α ignored rule-6 twins in half its own reporting → 0.709 → **0.721 [0.651, 0.787]**.
7. The 5 kcal floor was undisclosed under a "within 10%" label.

**No conclusion reversed.** The ML still earns nothing — and reads *more* cleanly afterwards, at
exactly level (5 wins each, p=1.000) rather than a noisy −3.9pp that was partly hash order.

### The test suite was the weakest part

50 mutations, **14 survived**. Two tests named the property they checked and then failed to check
it. All 14 are now closed, verified against a **passing control** — the first sweep reported all
14 caught and was wrong, because the sandbox omitted `scripts/` and `docs/` so collection errored
identically on every run. *A mutation sweep without a passing control measures whether the harness
is broken, not whether the tests discriminate.*

## 3. Phase 3, as built

```
bronze.raw_usda_portions   3.1  POST /v1/foods format=full, 20 ids/batch, resumable cache
  -> silver.usda_portions  3.2  modifier beats measureUnit; gramWeight/amount; fl oz ≠ oz
  -> silver.recipe_ingredient_grams
                           3.3  four paths: mass, pack_size, volumetric, count
  -> gram accuracy         3.3b measured against published reference weights  [NOT IN THE PLAN]
  -> gold.*                3.4  5 dbt models, staging → gated → published
  -> gold.ingredient_costs 3.5  98 entities, 75.4% of converted mass, a stand-in
  -> the gate              3.6  20 dbt tests, proven to block by injection
  -> dags/                 3.7  12 tasks, sequential, idempotent by content hash
```

**Design decisions inside the code**, each of which had a wrong-but-plausible alternative:

- **Nulls are never zeros.** A zero gram weight claims the ingredient weighs nothing and drags a
  recipe's total down while looking like a measurement.
- **`fl oz` is kept distinct from `oz`.** Conflating a volume with a mass would silently price
  every fluid-ounce portion at 28.35 g. The join misses instead — an honest gap.
- **The food's own weight for a measure beats deriving one.** Caught by a test: `from_volume`
  priced "1 tbsp flour" off the *cup* weight (125/16 = 7.81 g) when USDA publishes 7.8 g
  directly. Ratios are exact; weights are separately measured.
- **A declined pick is not an entity.** §12 fitted a threshold so the resolver could say "no
  confident match"; carrying its best guess into Gold would reintroduce the failure that
  threshold prevents.
- **`recipe_ingredients_resolved` keeps every line**, including unresolved and unweighed ones.
  Filtering would make a recipe missing four ingredients indistinguishable from a complete one.
- **Tags fire only at 100% coverage.** The ingredient we could not identify is exactly the one
  that might disqualify the recipe.
- **The gate is `dbt build`, not `dbt run` + `dbt test`.** One is a gate, the other an alarm.
  Asserted in three places so the decision survives its author.
- **Thresholds are set to the impossible, not the unusual.** The corpus genuinely contains 40 lb
  of pork fat and 13 gallons of ice cream; a gate that fires on real data gets muted.

## 4. Phase 3's review — and a claim of mine that was false

Three more agents. The headline finding was mine to own.

**I published that the entity-override gains were "not circular" and called +3.6pp kcal /
+9.2pp entity "the defensible numbers." That was wrong.** Excluding the 13 overridden strings,
both gains are **+0.0pp** — 100% came from `cinnamon` and `soda`, the only overridden strings in
the gold set, and the override sets each to *exactly* its gold `fdc_id`. My defence was about
label *provenance*; what makes it circular is that the intervention targeted the labelled item.
The bullet I wrote immediately after stated the mechanism and presented it as evidence against
itself.

Same for grams: the 23 non-overridden reference pairs are byte-identical before and after, so the
independent accuracy is **77.6%**, not 83.2%.

Four confirmed bugs, all fixed:

| bug | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| **false dietary claims** — four predicates matched *zero* rows | 40/429 gluten-free contained gluten; 9/170 vegan contained honey/gelatin/shrimp | **0/329, 0/147, 0/604** |
| pack-size multiplied an *equivalence* | "7 pt. (5 lb.) sugar" → 15,876 g | **2,268 g** |
| servings regex stepped over the unit | "Yield: 2 1/2 dozen" → 2 | **30** |
| per-serving mixed bases | 2,192 values, 94% from incomplete recipes | **118**, complete only |

And the gate claim was overstated: `dbt build` tests the *built* table, so the probe row genuinely
sat in `gold.canonical_ingredients`. Fixed by building the thing rather than rewording it —
staging → gate → transactional publish → run stamp → exported Gold-only artifact.

## 5. Mistakes I made, and how they were caught

Worth recording separately, because the pattern is consistent and it is the useful lesson.

| mistake | how it surfaced |
|---|---|
| Claimed the override gains were "not circular" | a reviewer recomputed with the overrides excluded |
| Said parser fixes would invalidate 300 gold labels (it was 11) | measured it before acting |
| Reported "3.1 still running" for over an hour | the watcher's `pgrep` matched **its own command line** and waited for itself |
| Estimated the portions pull at 15–25 min (it was ~25 but I mis-derived it) | timed a single-food request, not a 20-food batch |
| Wrote a mutation sweep that reported all 14 caught | ran a control; the sandbox was broken and every run failed identically |
| Asserted the distribution checks would catch the pack-size bug | checked: the **median does not move at all**; only p99 does (3.00×) |
| Documented a `servings` column I never implemented | a reviewer diffed the docstring against the schema |

Every one was caught by *measuring the claim* rather than re-reading it. The three that reached
published documentation (circularity, gate blocking, servings) all had the same shape: a
plausible statement written next to real work, never separately verified.

**The coverage compounding is Phase 3's real finding.** 63.9% per line looks healthy; 4.7% per
recipe is brutal, because a recipe needs *every* line. Phase 4 must either answer for that small
slice or answer broadly with coverage attached — a product decision worth making deliberately.

### Open, and recorded rather than dropped

- **`data_trust_score` is weakly interpretable.** Coverage counts *lines*, not mass: at 0.90
  coverage the p90 of actually-missing energy is 50%. Both factors are kept as their own columns,
  which is the mitigation.
- **`_weight_for`'s median fallback fabricates weights** USDA never published, on ~10% of
  converted lines (`brown sugar` = 182.5 g is neither packed 220 nor loose 145).
- **Residual head errors** — `coconut` still resolves to coconut oil; `white sugar` → `Sugars, brown`.
- **The 300 gold labels remain 94% LLM-produced.** Every accuracy figure measures agreement with
  them, not with ground truth.
